1. **Hard ordering, not a sub-step.** The brief states as a top-level rule: *you may not call

## 3.5 Why fixes (a) and (b) only co-exist in the "near-but-not-touching" regime (the review correction)

The first draft split the two fixes across the pre-approach look: log a NEAR-or-`FAR` there (prong a),
and invoke the macro "from distance / a visible gap" (prong b). The 2026-07-04 adversarial review found
these contradict: coverage (prong a) needs a NEAR at the approach's **start** step — only a claim at or
before the drop covers a contact-drop landing at the macro's END (`_coverage`: `n <= s`). But if "invoke
from distance" means the suspect is NOT near at the start, the honest pre-approach claim is "far", which
covers nothing — reproducing v3's starved `q_k` while technically "complying." The `FAR` escape line made
this loophole explicit.

**Resolution (decision of record).** The suspect must be **NEAR** — visible inside the tracked
`read_region` box, so the pre-approach NEAR is honest and covers the imminent contact-drop — AND have a
**gap TO CONTACT large enough for the approach to take at least 3 presses.** "Distance invocation" (prong
b) was never "suspect far/invisible"; it means "enough gap to contact," fully compatible with
"near/visible."

**The binding arithmetic (corrected after the second review — the first draft had the emphasis
backwards).** For `approach_suspect` (a `repeat_until` over a 1-press inner list) the executor makes
`iterations == executed_step_count == world_steps_used == k` (verified against all 12 approach records in
`runs/brain_kirby_v3/world/skills.jsonl`), so the skill guard's two clauses collapse to a **single
binding constraint: `k >= 3`** (`executed_step_count >= 3`; `iterations >= 2` is then automatic).
Coverage needs the drop at `n+k` covered by the pre-approach NEAR at `n`, i.e. `k <= 15` — **essentially
free** (an approach never walks 15 presses of `hold_frames=30`). So the real regime is `3 <= k <= 15` and
**the LOWER bound is what bites:** the gap must be big enough that `approach_suspect` takes **at least 3
full presses** (each `right`, `hold_frames=30` ≈ 46 frames, a large screen-space step) before
`region_changed` fires at contact. The first draft's "keep the gap moderate so the span stays ≤ W"
framing was the wrong worry — the span being too SHORT (guard floor), not too LONG (coverage), is the
risk. **v3's fatal undershoot:** the max `k` ever observed across the whole run was **2** (steps 28, 55 —
the verdict's "one short of the floor"); every other approach fired at `k=1`, because approaches were
invoked at adjacency. A near-and-visible suspect with only "a few tiles" of gap reproduces exactly those
k≤2 distances — so the brief demands a gap sized for **≥3 presses**, stated as a number, not "a few
tiles." v3 sat OUTSIDE this regime on both prongs at once: NEARs logged after contact (no coverage) and
approaches from adjacency (no 3-press loop). The `FAR` line is removed; the covering claim is always a
NEAR (§3 lever 1).

## 4. Fix (b): gap-to-contact invocation (decision of record)

Prong (b) is genuinely NEW guidance — the v3 brief never told the brain WHERE to stand when invoking the
macro — so it is not an anti-thrash repeat.

**The v3.1 brief change:**
1. **Approach only across a gap big enough for ≥3 presses.** Per §3.5's arithmetic (`k >= 3` is the
   binding floor): invoke `approach_suspect` only when the suspect is near/visible in your latest
   `read_region` box AND far enough that reaching contact needs **at least 3 full `right` presses**
   (`hold_frames=30`, each a large step). The brief states this as the number 3, not "a few tiles" —
   because "a few tiles" is exactly what produced `k≤2` (never 3) in all 12 of v3's approach calls.
2. **State why, in the scorer's terms.** The brief explains: a skill counts as real evidence only if its
   `stop_when` fires after the loop genuinely repeated. Invoking at contact-adjacency fires
   `region_changed` on press 1 — a one-shot dressed as a loop, which the scorer rejects.
3. **Drops are the priority — no unbounded retreat loop.** If the suspect is ALREADY touching (contact
   imminent on press 1), the brief says: do NOT loop-retreat trying to manufacture a gap — just take the
   contact and bank the drop, then re-position. The gap discipline governs only when the brain HAS a
   choice; it never defers a drop it can bank. (This closes the review's finding #2 — an unbounded
   "retreat until a gap exists" rule against an enemy that re-approaches could have starved drops below
   `MIN_SESSION_DROPS = 5`.)
4. **Auditability.** The pre-approach NEAR line may carry an optional trailing `gap=<n>tiles` note
   (`remember "NEAR id=<k> step=<n> gap=3tiles"`). The `_NEAR_RE` regex (`NEAR\s+id=..\s+step=..`,
   `.search`) matches the prefix and ignores the suffix, so this stays machinery-frozen while making the
   gap the brain judged auditable post-hoc — addressing the "several tiles is unquantified" review nit.

**Honest bound (sharpened after the second review — the binding floor is `k >= 3`, not `>= 2`):** the
guard's real bar is `executed_step_count >= 3`, and in the ENTIRE v3 run no `approach_suspect` call ever
reached `k = 3` (max was 2, twice). A larger gap raises `k`, but nothing proves a distance-invoked
approach reliably clears 3 on real Kirby enemy movement — a fast-closing enemy could still fire
`region_changed` before the 3rd press, and the symmetric risk (a fast enemy re-closing the gap so clean
big-gap invocations are rare) could suppress qualifying-conditional calls AND, if the brain over-defers,
drops. These remain the hypotheses the one paid run tests; §7 names both INSUFFICIENT_DATA (guard) and
`INSUFFICIENT_DROPS` as possible fix-(b) outcomes.

> **v3.2-(b) escalation (pre-registered, NOT built here — the symmetric partner to §3's (a) guard).** IF
> v3.1 again lands INSUFFICIENT_DATA on the skill guard because `k` never reaches 3 (a large-gap brief
> still undershoots — `region_changed` fires early on real enemy movement), v3.2 adds a stricter-only
> executor floor: `approach_suspect`'s `repeat_until` evaluates `stop_when` only AFTER a minimum of 3
> inner presses (a `min_iters = 3` guard), so a genuine approach always logs `k >= 3`. Stricter-only (the
> loop can only run longer, never shorter) — a code build with its own free gate. Coverage is preserved:
> a contact landing at press 1 of a forced 3-press span is still covered by the pre-approach NEAR at the
> span start (`n <= n+1`), and macro-interior claim exclusion is unaffected (it excludes CLAIMS naming
> interior steps, not the drops themselves). This closes the review's observation that only prong (a) had
> a pre-registered fallback.
